# Allowance snapshots and learning from blockers

Proposed by Michael; recorded by Codex/Sol for Icarus's shared learning.

## Executor failure

Allowance control was already an owner priority. The executor waited for Michael to propose automatic usage snapshots instead of investigating available allowance readers proactively. Discussion of uncertain historical availability then sidestepped that failure of initiative. The corrective action is discovery and execution, followed by durable learning and readback.

Michael's exact principle: **"A blocker is not a blocker. It's a data point."** Learn from the failure and go around, through, or over it within the authorized scope. Record the observed failure, what changed, the materially different route, and the resulting evidence. Do not retry blindly or substitute an apology for action.

## Reusable snapshot procedure

1. Identify the actual provider, account meter and reset window. Inspect existing authorized tools, relevant training and prior attempts before claiming the allowance cannot be read.
2. Test the provider's own usage reader or readable usage page. Capture percentage used/remaining, observation time, reset time and source. Preserve separate meters and windows; missing data is unknown.
3. Save a latest snapshot and append dated observations to a history. Keep credentials, account identifiers and unrelated private information out of the record.
4. Schedule the tested reader at the cadence Michael authorizes. Verify both the saved schedule and an actual automatic run.
5. Keep quiet while unchanged or non-actionable. On failure, retain the last valid observation, mark it stale, diagnose the failure signature and use a materially different valid route.
6. Distinguish observed readings from estimates and account-wide usage from usage attributable to one task. Do not pool providers' balances or consume reset credits/purchase credits as part of monitoring.
